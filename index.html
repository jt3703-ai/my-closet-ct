How the rules work · assumptions & limitations

Each outfit has one top, one pair of trousers, outerwear, and shoes. Items are available, fit the wearer, and are clean. The wardrobe and today's situation are invented.

Rules flag floor-length hems in rain, non-resistant outerwear or shoes in rain, low-warmth tops below 16°C, and shoes without a walking label on walks of 45 minutes or more. These thresholds and labels are illustrative assumptions, not validated facts.

Alternative matching uses silhouette for silhouette reasons and a fabric-feel label for feel reasons. For “It matches the outfit better,” the system accepts your judgment about the overall outfit; practical alternatives do not require shared color or style labels and are not claims of aesthetic compatibility. It cannot determine what you actually find stylish or comfortable. An alternative must be owned, in the same category, and have fewer flagged concerns; otherwise none is suggested. For the outfit-match reason, alternatives are ranked by fewer concerns, with wardrobe order breaking ties; no style score is used. Your replacement is never automatically changed.

All three reasons use prepared text. The feedback evaluates the updated outfit, including concerns in other pieces. Refresh to restore the initial recommendation; changes are not saved. No uploads, shopping, external APIs, or AI analysis.
